Old-School Batting Order

1. Leadoff: a fast runner who steals bases and hits for a good average, with little power. His job is to get on base and into scoring position.

2. Second: a contact hitter who rarely strikes out and can bunt or hit-and-run to move the leadoff man along.

3. Third: the best all-around hitter on the team, usually the highest batting average with some power.

4. Cleanup: the top power hitter, the team leader in home runs and RBI.

5. Fifth: the second-best power hitter. He bats behind the cleanup man so pitchers cannot pitch around him.

6. Sixth: the best of the remaining hitters.

7. Seventh: the next best of the remaining hitters.

8. Eighth: one of the weakest position players, often the catcher.

9. Ninth: the weakest hitter (the pitcher, before the universal DH). Some managers bat a second fast runner here as a "second leadoff" man.
